Compare organic and paid performance. Keep one or two winners, replace weak hooks, and create controlled variants from the best footage.

### Weeks 3–4 — Scale only what converts

Increase spend only when the ad produces completed Family registrations at an acceptable cost and the registrations are activating in the product.

## 11. Numbers explained simply

Think of the marketing funnel as a row of doors:

```text
Person sees ad
→ clicks landing page
→ creates free account
→ actually attends/watches
→ becomes a paying family
→ stays for months or years
```

### Key terms

- **Ad spend:** the money paid to Facebook.
- **Click:** someone visits the landing page.
- **Free registration:** a Family account is created.
- **Activation:** the family actually uses the product, such as attending class or watching a lesson.
- **Paid customer:** the family begins paying $67/month.
- **Conversion rate:** the percentage that moves through one door to the next.
- **CPA:** cost per action. Always name the action: registration CPA, activation CPA, etc.
- **CAC:** customer acquisition cost — ad spend divided by the number of new paying families.
- **LTV:** lifetime value — the contribution expected from one paying family over the time it stays.
- **Payback period:** how many paid months are needed to earn back the CAC.

### Easy example

Suppose we spend **$400**.

- 100 people click.
- 20 create free accounts.
- 5 actually use the class.
- 2 become paying families.

Then:

- cost per click = $400 ÷ 100 = **$4**;
- free-registration CPA = $400 ÷ 20 = **$20**;
- paid CAC = $400 ÷ 2 = **$200**;
- landing-to-registration conversion = 20 out of 100 = **20%**;
- free-to-paid conversion = 2 out of 20 = **10%**.

The most important distinction is:

- **$200 per paying family** may be workable.
- **$200 per free account** would usually be extremely expensive.

At the planning assumption of about **$56.95 monthly contribution** from a $67 subscription, a $200 CAC is earned back after about **3.5 paid months**. If the average family stays 12 paid months, estimated contribution is about **$683**, or approximately **3.4 times** the $200 CAC. If families really stay for years, a high initial CAC can still be rational.

### The formula that matters

```text
Paid CAC = free-registration CPA ÷ free-to-paid conversion rate
```

Examples:

- $20 registration CPA ÷ 10% paid conversion = $200 paid CAC.
- $40 registration CPA ÷ 20% paid conversion = $200 paid CAC.
- $60 registration CPA ÷ 30% paid conversion = $200 paid CAC.

We therefore need to track both the price of a free signup and the percentage that becomes paid.

## 12. Does management make sense before ads?

Yes. This is the correct time to organize it.

Before money is spent, establish:

- stable creative IDs;
- exact source/clip history;
- landing-page tracking;
- registration and activation definitions;
- a simple scorecard;
- a posting calendar;
- one place for results.

Without that system, the first ad dollars produce anecdotes. With it, even a small test teaches us which hook, clip, and audience produced a registration.

## 13. Initial paid-budget rule

Start with a bounded learning budget after the first 4–7 days of organic publishing:

- approximately $300–$500 total;
- approximately $30–$50 per day total;
- approximately three provisional winning creatives;
- same audience and landing page;
- one main creative variable changed at a time.

This first test identifies useful creatives and registration cost. It does not yet prove long-term CAC or LTV.
